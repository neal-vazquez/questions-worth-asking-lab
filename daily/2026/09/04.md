# Release rubric for AI-assisted research notes

**Date:** 2026-09-04  
**Area:** Responsible AI / research workflow  
**Status:** Working pattern, not an empirical result

## Question

What is the smallest review process that can keep a short AI-assisted research note useful, honest, and safe to publish?

## Five-dimension rubric

Score each dimension from 0 to 2 before release.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Factual grounding | Unsupported or contradicted | Plausible but incompletely checked | Supported by an identified source or directly reproducible reasoning |
| Traceability | Origin is unclear | Some sources or transformations are visible | Claims, sources, and transformations can be followed |
| Epistemic labeling | Fact, inference, and opinion are blended | Some uncertainty is marked | Observation, inference, hypothesis, and opinion are clearly distinguished |
| Public safety | Contains unresolved sensitive material | Low apparent risk but not explicitly checked | Reviewed for privacy, confidentiality, credentials, and identifying details |
| Decision utility | No clear use | Interesting but weakly actionable | Helps a reader decide, test, compare, or investigate something |

## Proposed release rule

Publish only when:

1. no dimension scores **0**;
2. the total score is at least **8 out of 10**; and
3. any factual claim that materially affects the conclusion has a visible verification path.

A passing score is not proof that a note is correct. It is a lightweight gate that makes avoidable failures less likely and leaves an audit trail for later revision.

## Why this structure

The dimensions separate different failure modes. A note can be factually accurate but unsafe to publish. It can be well sourced but useless. It can be insightful while disguising inference as observation. Collapsing these concerns into a single “quality” score hides the reason a draft needs work.

The rule also favors revision over false precision. If a draft cannot pass, the next action should be concrete: verify a claim, expose a source, relabel uncertainty, remove sensitive material, or explain the note's intended use.

## Minimal review record

A compact record can travel with the draft:

```yaml
grounding: 2
traceability: 1
epistemic_labeling: 2
public_safety: 2
decision_utility: 1
total: 8
review_note: "Publishable as a working pattern; add source links if factual examples are introduced."
```

This rubric is intentionally small enough to use repeatedly. Its value should be judged by whether it catches meaningful problems without turning every short note into a compliance exercise.
